# Clarify Brief: clarify-research-loop (iteration 3)

**Slug:** clarify-research-loop
**Timestamp:** 20260412T011953Z
**Status:** draft
**Complexity:** complex

> **Iteration 3 of N** — supersedes the iter-2 brief at `20260412T001619Z`. Both prior dossiers and the deferred-gaps backlog inform this iteration. The reframe is philosophical, not mechanical: iter 2 had the right mechanisms but lacked a strong model of what the loop converges *toward*. Iter 3 names that model: a **terminal-state taxonomy** with seven valid resolutions, of which "produce a spec" is only one. Convergence is defined as the set of possible terminals narrowing to one — not as the LLM judging the brief good enough.

## Idea

> Verbatim from owner across iterations 2 and 3 (combined):

(iter 2) I'm looking to refactor this process a bit to be more epistemologically interesting as a human-in-the-loop observer. I want to use research to learn things — and then maybe understand my original understanding was misguided and so I reclarify — but not lose the research. And also during research we are surfacing new things we didn't think about, so we could be blending things. What's the optimal way of doing this to stay consistent? So we have little loops, and we run little tests and stuff and build things to decision gate.

(iter 3) What feels off though is that I don't see a really strong vision of how these multiple loops are trending us towards a resolution. We need a stronger model of the different resolutions that we could be going towards with clarify and research phases. Obviously research is gated before spec and that's good, but we need a strong vision and philosophy for what we are actually doing with this loop.

## Goal

Cortex's pre-spec phase becomes a **terminal-state finder**: a learning loop whose job is to converge a slug to one of seven valid resolution states (Commit-to-Build, Kill-with-Learning, Decompose, Experiment-Required, Already-Exists, Hold-on-Dependency, or transitional Reframe-and-Continue), where convergence is defined as the set of possible terminals mechanically narrowing to one across iterations, where research and small bounded tests are the primary uncertainty-reduction tools, where prior learning is preserved across reframings via versioned-with-cross-references artifacts, and where the human-as-observer can always answer two questions about any slug: "what do I currently understand?" and "which terminal am I heading toward?"

---

## Philosophical Core: The Seven Terminal States

The single most important addition in iteration 3. The loop is a **terminal-state finder**, not a spec-generator. A slug that ends in Kill after three iterations with clear evidence is a **success of the loop**, not a failure. The loop's job is to converge to the *correct* terminal — whichever it is — not to push every slug toward Build.

| # | Terminal | Meaning | Commit Action | Artifact Produced |
|---|----------|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Commit-to-Build** | Frame stable, critical uncertainties resolved, scope clear, value justified | `/cortex-spec` | `spec.md` + `contract-001.md` |
| 2 | **Kill-with-Learning** | Research showed this isn't worth doing; preserve the *why* | `/cortex-close --terminal kill` | `kill-rationale.md` + facts to facts.jsonl |
| 3 | **Decompose** | What looked like one slug is actually N child slugs | `/cortex-decompose` (new) | `decomposition.md` listing child slug names |
| 4 | **Experiment-Required** | A critical uncertainty cannot be resolved by reading and synthesis alone | `/cortex-experiment open` | `learning-contract-{id}.md` |
| 5 | **Already-Exists** | The desired capability already exists; activate or use it instead of building | `/cortex-close --terminal use-existing` | `use-existing.md` pointing at the existing thing |
| 6 | **Hold-on-Dependency** | Resolution depends on something external (another slug, decision, time) | `/cortex-hold` (new) | `hold-trigger.md` with explicit re-entry condition |
| 7 | **Reframe-and-Continue** | Minor frame shift; iterate at N+1 with updated brief | `/cortex-clarify` (new iteration) | New brief with `supersedes:` |

The first six are **terminal**: the slug ends there. The seventh is **transitional**: it loops back into the same slug at iteration N+1.

### Convergence model: terminal-set narrowing

At each iteration, the set of *possible* terminals should narrow.

- **Iter 1 (just clarified, no research yet):** all 6 non-transitional terminals are possible
- **Iter 2 (after first research pass):** evidence rules some out (e.g., a thorough prior-art search with no hits rules out Already-Exists; surfaced heterogeneity adds Decompose to the table)
- **Iter N:** the set continues to narrow as evidence accumulates
- **Convergence:** the set has reduced to exactly one terminal — the loop has reached resolution

This is mechanical and auditable. The current-understanding.md doc tracks the live terminal set in a section called **Possible Terminals**, with one ruled-out reason per eliminated terminal. That section is the loop's progress meter — a human can read it and see "we started with 6, we are down to 2, we are converging."

The brief declares its **initial terminal set** in YAML frontmatter (see this brief's frontmatter, lines 13-19). Most slugs declare all six as possible. Some can pre-rule-out: e.g., a slug declared as exploratory might only allow `{experiment-required, kill-with-learning}`, signaling that Commit is not in scope. Pre-narrowing the set is a form of declared epistemic honesty.

### Why this matters

Current Cortex has a hidden assumption: every slug will eventually be built. Every mechanism is designed to push toward spec. The dormant discovery loop is a perfect demonstration of why this is wrong — a slug whose correct resolution is "this already exists, activate it" cannot be served by a pipeline that only knows how to produce specs. The terminal-state model is the difference between **a build pipeline that allows deviations** (current Cortex) and **a discovery loop with multiple legitimate exits** (what this slug is designing).

## Non-Goals

- Not a project management tool or Gantt-chart system
- Not replacing `/cortex-spec` — spec still exists; it just becomes one of seven commit actions, not the privileged one
- Not enforcing a fixed loop structure — the loop should be sized to the question
- Not building a knowledge graph or semantic memory layer
- Not eliminating commitment — there is still a moment of reaching a terminal; indecision is itself a failure mode
- Not retrofitting all closed slugs
- Not removing or replacing the existing dormant `reclarify_required` / `cortex-experiment` machinery — subsume, not delete
- Not solving the multi-slug case (cross-slug learning is a separate concern)
- **Not requiring all six new terminal artifacts to ship at once** — pilot the model first as a layer on top of `/cortex-close --terminal {name}` that just records which terminal was reached; add the per-terminal artifacts (decomposition.md, hold-trigger.md, use-existing.md) only if/when slugs actually reach those terminals
- **Not introducing a new "decision verdict UI"** that interrupts every slug; the terminal set narrowing is computed mechanically and surfaced in current-understanding.md, not as an interactive prompt

## Constraints

- [owner-intent] No code without contract
- [owner-intent] Validators must run
- [owner-intent] Disk is truth — every preserved learning must be a repo artifact
- [owner-intent] GSD owns execution
- [owner-intent] No sycophantic evals
- **Research findings must accumulate, not be overwritten** when a slug reframes
- **Reframings must be explicit and traceable** via `supersedes:` / `informed_by:` / `reframe_reason:` frontmatter
- **Little tests must be cheap and bounded** — appetite measured in minutes-to-hours, not days
- **Provisional vs durable knowledge must be distinguishable** in the artifact schema
- **Coherence is queryable** via current-understanding.md
- **Convergence is mechanical, not LLM-judged** — the terminal set narrows by evidence, not by feeling
- **The spec command is no longer privileged** — Commit-to-Build is one of seven terminals; the loop's success metric is reaching the *right* terminal, not reaching spec
- **Each non-Commit terminal must produce an artifact** that preserves the learning (kill-rationale.md, decomposition.md, use-existing.md, hold-trigger.md, learning-contract.md). Killed slugs are not lost slugs — they are slugs whose value was the learning.
- **The terminal-state model must be testable as a layer first** — adding `--terminal {name}` to `/cortex-close` is the minimum viable pilot; full per-terminal commands can come later
- **Iteration must be bounded by terminal convergence**, not by max-iteration count alone — the loop ends when the terminal set has size 1, regardless of iteration number; max-iteration acts as a circuit-breaker only

## Assumptions

- The owner values epistemic honesty over delivery speed — being wrong and learning is preferable to shipping fast on a bad frame
- Most insights worth preserving come from research surprises, not from confirming the brief
- The seven terminals listed are exhaustive for current Cortex use cases — but the model should be extensible if a new terminal emerges
- The single most common terminal will remain Commit-to-Build (~50% of slugs); the value of the model is in correctly handling the other ~50% rather than in changing the dominant case
- The terminal-set narrowing rule can be made mechanical for at least 4 of the 6 terminals (Already-Exists ruled out by negative prior-art search; Experiment-Required surfaces when a question is marked `resolution_path: experiment`; Decompose surfaces when research finds N independent sub-problems; Hold surfaces when a finding names an external blocker). The remaining 2 (Commit and Kill) are the harder cases requiring judgment.
- The pilot of the model can succeed with just `--terminal {name}` flags on `/cortex-close`, deferring per-terminal commands and artifacts until a slug actually needs them
- The brief's `initial_terminal_set` and `ruled_out` frontmatter fields can be added without breaking existing briefs (additive)
- The current-understanding.md doc's "Possible Terminals" section is the loop's progress meter; the rest of its content (durable findings, provisional thoughts, open questions) is supporting context
- The owner expects the system to push back when terminals are being incorrectly ruled in or out — politeness is a failure mode
