## Summary

The epistemic-loop framing has a strong external precedent — **Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), as articulated by Michael Nygard and Martin Fowler** — and Cortex already has most of the substrate to implement it (YAML frontmatter with `supersedes`/`informed_by` fields, timestamped artifact naming, the dormant `reclarify_required` machinery, an unused structured uncertainty register, the `cortex-experiment` skill, and the `facts.jsonl` knowledge engine). The work is **not** building new infrastructure from scratch; it is **composing existing primitives into a coherent loop** plus closing nine specific gaps identified in the codebase audit. The single highest-leverage gap is **a per-slug `current-understanding.md` artifact** that aggregates findings across iterations and dossier phases — without it, the human-as-observer cannot answer "what do I currently know?" and reframings will continue to either erase prior learning or pile up unread artifacts. The blending strategy that best preserves epistemic honesty (per both ADR practice and Perplexity's comparison synthesis) is **versioned-with-explicit-cross-references**, not overwrite and not pure append-only. Stopping criteria should follow the **stage-gate decision matrix** model: Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle as explicit outcomes, with a deterministic pass threshold based on "must-meet" criteria — adapted to Cortex as a small concrete checklist (frame-stable / critical-uncertainties-resolved / micro-experiments-converged) rather than a confidence score. Micro-experiments (appetite < 1 hour) are a real research methodology, not a Cortex invention — the existing `cortex-experiment` learning-contract format is composable but its interactive validation ceremony makes it too heavy for sub-hour tests; a `--quick` variant is needed. Most importantly: the new design must **avoid the failure mode of the existing dormant discovery loop**, which is "designed but never triggered." That means concrete triggers (not soft prose), an explicit invocation pattern (not implicit hope), and a pilot on one slug (not a top-down rollout).

## Findings

### Existing Cortex substrate (codebase audit, q1)

- **Cortex already has 9 of the building blocks** for an epistemic loop, but they are mostly disconnected: `cortex-experiment` skill (full lifecycle, 332 lines, never invoked); `cortex-stash` (transient idea capture, never linked to durable artifacts); `reclarify_required` field (read by 4 skills, never written in practice); structured uncertainty register schema in `open-questions.md` (read by `/cortex-spec` for the critical-uncertainty gate, never written by any skill); `facts.jsonl` knowledge engine (read by `/cortex-research`, `/cortex-drive`, `/cortex-investigate` — but **no skill writes facts after research synthesis**); timestamped versioned clarify briefs with `supersedes`/`informed_by`/`reframe_reason` fields (used in *this slug's* iteration 2, but no other slug uses them); per-phase research dossiers (one per phase, never cross-referenced across phases); the YAML frontmatter convention; the autonomy gate system.
- **The gap is not infrastructure; the gap is composition.** No skill currently aggregates dossiers, no skill writes to `facts.jsonl` after research, no skill produces a single "current understanding" view, no skill resolves entries in the structured uncertainty register, and no convention exists for distinguishing provisional thoughts from durable findings within a dossier.
- **The one slug currently using iteration metadata is `clarify-research-loop` itself** (this slug). The iteration-2 brief at `20260412T001619Z` has explicit `iteration: 2`, `supersedes:`, `informed_by:`, and `reframe_reason:` frontmatter. No other slug in the repo uses this pattern — `repo-consistency-fixes` had two briefs but neither referenced the other. The convention exists in exactly one place: the slug that invented it for itself.
- **`cortex-experiment` learning-contract is composable but heavy.** The contract format (5 required fields at open + 5 at close) is too ceremonial for sub-hour tests. The Appetite/Timebox field is REQUIRED but unstructured — no schema for what "30 min" or "2 hours" means operationally. There is no `--quick` variant or lightweight subcommand.
- **`/cortex-research` Phase 4 reclarify trigger is single-sentence soft prose.** Per the dossier from iteration 1, this is why the gate has never fired. The soft trigger is partly a deliberate design choice (per owner-intent: "no LLM-judged 'is this brief good enough' gates" — sycophancy risk), but the result is that the LLM running research never notices when its own findings should reframe the brief.
- **Dossiers are silo'd by phase, not cumulative.** A slug with `concept-*.md` + `implementation-*.md` + `evals/eval-proposal.md` has its knowledge scattered across three files with no aggregation layer. The reader (human or `/cortex-spec`) must read all three and reconcile them mentally.

### External: Architecture Decision Records (q2, q5, q7)

- **The ADR pattern (Michael Nygard / Martin Fowler) is a near-perfect mechanical match** for the user's epistemic-loop requirements. ADRs are: (a) short — a couple of pages; (b) one file per decision, monotonically numbered; (c) status enum `proposed → accepted → superseded`; (d) **once accepted, never reopened or changed — instead superseded with an explicit link**; (e) contain rationale, alternatives considered, and consequences; (f) recommend recording the "confidence level" of the decision and "changes in product context that should trigger the team to reevaluate."
- **The "trigger to reevaluate" field in ADRs is exactly what Cortex's reclarify trigger should be.** Instead of asking the LLM at synthesis time "did the frame change?", you make the *prior brief* declare in advance "I should be reevaluated if X, Y, or Z is observed in research." The new dossier then mechanically checks against those triggers — concrete signals, not soft prose.
- **ADRs explicitly use `superseded` as a first-class status.** This is the Cortex equivalent of moving an old brief or dossier to a "superseded" state with a link forward to the new one — preserving the audit trail without confusing readers about which is current.
- **Lab notebook practice (TowardsDataScience, 2022; russpoldrack 2025) treats the notebook as "the primary record of a researcher" — hypotheses, experiments, and initial interpretation.** Lab notebooks are intentionally distinct from published papers: notebooks contain provisional thinking and dead ends; papers contain durable findings. This is the structural answer to q8 (provisional vs durable): two different artifact types with different lifetimes and different audit standards. Cortex already has this distinction implicitly (`cortex-stash` ≈ provisional, dossier ≈ durable) — it just needs a third tier for "in-progress research notes within a slug."
- **Jupyter best practices (Russ Poldrack, 2025): "shorter notebooks targeted at specific functions" beats "one big beautiful notebook."** This is the structural answer to "little loops": don't try to make one giant research pass cover everything; make many small focused passes that each close one uncertainty. The dossier-per-phase pattern Cortex already has is closer to this than to the monolith — the gap is making the passes *smaller and faster* (micro-experiments) and giving them a way to *roll up* into a current-understanding view.

### Stopping criteria and decision gates (q6)

- **Stage-gate / phase-gate models (Cooper, R&D process literature)** define a decision gate as a structured checkpoint where stakeholders evaluate progress against pre-defined criteria and produce one of four explicit outcomes: **Go, Kill, Hold, or Recycle**. The "Recycle" outcome maps directly to "go back and reframe" — making it a first-class option rather than a failure mode.
- **Pass threshold for stage-gate is "must-meet" criteria + scored matrix exceeding benchmarks.** Translated to Cortex: the decision gate from research → spec must check (a) concrete must-meets (frame stable, no critical uncertainties open, all assumptions backed) and (b) the human's confidence (override possible in either direction). The must-meets are deterministic; the confidence is where human judgment lives.
- **Stage-gate practice for early stages is "light-touch to filter ideas quickly."** Translated: the early gates in the epistemic loop should be cheap to pass for slugs that don't need iteration (don't punish the 80% case), and tighten only when reframings are actively in progress.
- **The stopping criteria comparison from iteration 1 still holds:** combine max iterations + delta threshold + circuit breaker. The new framing adds one element: the human's "Recycle" decision is itself a valid loop continuation — the loop only stops when the human says Go (commit to spec), Kill (close the slug), or Hold (park it). Indecision is not a stopping state.

### Blending strategies (q5)

- **Versioned documents with explicit cross-references is the strongest strategy** by Perplexity's synthesis across lab notebook, design doc, and engineering decision log practice. The reasoning: it preserves the audit trail of *why* you changed your mind, supports falsifiability (you can compare what you used to think to what you think now), and matches verification practices in research rigor.
- **Overwrite is rejected** as breaking auditability — it hides errors and discarded ideas, preventing verification of decision quality.
- **Append-only is rated moderate** — preserves chronology but lacks structure for linking updates to prior entries; risks information overload and buried insights without explicit cross-references.
- **Cortex's existing `supersedes`/`informed_by` frontmatter convention is exactly the cross-reference layer the versioned strategy requires.** It just needs to be applied consistently across all artifact types (briefs, dossiers, contracts, eval proposals) and enforced — not optional.

### Micro-experiments (q3)

- **Micro-experiment is a real research methodology**, not a Cortex coinage. Per the Perplexity synthesis: "small-scale, low-overhead test designed to quickly gather insights on a specific idea or approach... results obtained rapidly — typically under an hour." Concrete examples that match the user's wording: read one paper, call one API, prompt one LLM, hand-classify 20-50 items.
- **The key distinction from full hypothesis-driven experiments** is *scale* (small/fast) and *goal* (directional signal, not statistical significance). The output of a micro-experiment is "viable / not viable / need more info" — exactly the kind of signal that closes a single uncertainty in a research pass.
- **The existing `cortex-experiment` learning-contract is the right vehicle** but needs sizing-down. The contract requires a hypothesis ("We believe X will result in Y because Z"), a learning threshold, and an appetite — all sensible for micro-experiments. What needs to drop: the interactive 5-field validation ceremony at open and another at close, and the heavy "Risks & Dependencies" / "Expected Learning" sections. A `--quick` flag could skip these and use defaults.

### What did I not consider (deep-depth wait check)

- **Adoption is the real risk, not design.** The dormant discovery loop is the proof: a perfectly designed mechanism that nobody invokes is worse than a clunky one that gets used. The new design must explicitly address how it avoids the same fate. Pilot-on-one-slug-first is the safest answer; making the new flow the *default* path (not opt-in) is a stronger one.
- **Tension between "small focused notebooks" and "single current understanding."** Jupyter best practice says many small notebooks; the user's request implies one queryable view. Resolution: many small artifacts (research passes, micro-experiment results, dossier phases) feeding one synthesized current-understanding doc — not one or the other. The synthesis is computed/regenerated, not manually maintained.
- **What does a critic say?** "You're proposing nine new artifact types and skills. The previous slug shipped 5 scripts and 1 SKILL over 10 days. This is 3-5x larger. You should radically scope down — pick the three highest-leverage gaps and ship those, leave the rest for future slugs." This critique is correct and is reflected in the recommendations below.
- **Favorable conditions:** the user is actively engaged with the philosophy and reframed twice in two days — they will use what they help design. ADR pattern is standard and translates mechanically. Cortex already has YAML frontmatter, timestamping, and supersedes conventions — the substrate exists. The slug currently being researched (this one) is itself the working example — a meta-pilot.

## Trade-offs

### Option: Top-down redesign — ship all 9 gap closures in one slug
**Pros:** Coherent end state; no half-built intermediate states; the design hangs together; can be evaluated as a whole.
**Cons:** Large scope (3-5x prior slugs); high risk of partial completion; reproduces the dormant-loop failure if any part is unused; encourages over-engineering.
**Verdict:** rejected.

### Option: Three-gap minimum viable epistemic loop
**Pros:** Closes the highest-leverage gaps first (current-understanding artifact, fact-extraction pipeline, micro-experiment quick variant); each is independently useful; allows dogfooding before committing to the rest; matches the "smallest unit that reduces uncertainty meaningfully" Shape Up principle from iteration 1.
**Cons:** Leaves the structured uncertainty register dormant for now; doesn't immediately solve the "iteration history" or "dossier consolidation" problems; requires a follow-up slug.
**Verdict:** **selected.**

### Option: Activate the existing dormant infrastructure first, defer new artifacts
**Pros:** Zero new artifact types; pure operationalization; lowest possible scope; tests whether the existing design actually works before extending it.
**Cons:** Doesn't address the user's stated philosophical shift (current-understanding view, micro-experiments, blending) — it only re-runs iteration 1; risks missing the actual epistemic improvement.
**Verdict:** rejected as primary, but the *activation* component (concrete reframe triggers, structured-uncertainty-register writes) should be folded into the selected option.

### Option: New `cortex-current` skill that synthesizes current-understanding on demand
**Pros:** No new artifact stored on disk to maintain; always fresh; computed from existing dossiers + briefs + facts; lowest persistence overhead; decoupled from research workflow.
**Cons:** Recomputation cost on every invocation; output not auditable without re-running; harder to diff "what changed in my understanding between iter1 and iter2."
**Verdict:** rejected. Prefer a written artifact (`current-understanding.md` per slug) updated incrementally, with the audit trail in git history. Cortex's "disk is truth" constraint argues for written, not computed.

### Option: Versioned `current-understanding.md` per iteration
**Pros:** Each major reframe writes a new version with `supersedes:` link, matching the ADR pattern; preserves the full audit trail; the human can diff versions to see "what I learned."
**Cons:** Adds one more artifact per slug; requires discipline to update; bulk for slugs that converge in one pass.
**Verdict:** **selected** — but only when iteration > 1. The single-pass case writes one current-understanding.md and never updates it, matching Pattern A from the prior chat exchange.

### Option: ADR-style status field on dossiers (`proposed | accepted | superseded`)
**Pros:** Direct ADR pattern adoption; gives readers a clear signal of which dossier is current; supports "linked supersession" without overwriting prior content; tooling can filter to "show me only accepted dossiers for this slug."
**Cons:** New field adds maintenance burden; changes existing dossier template (touched by every slug going forward).
**Verdict:** **selected.** Add `status:` to dossier YAML frontmatter with values `proposed | accepted | superseded`; default `accepted` for one-shot slugs.

### Option: Stage-gate Go/Kill/Hold/Recycle as explicit verdicts at the research → spec boundary
**Pros:** Forces a deliberate decision moment; the human cannot drift past the gate; "Recycle" makes reframing a first-class outcome instead of a failure mode; matches stage-gate literature with proven track record.
**Cons:** Adds friction to the happy path; risks the human always picking "Go" without thinking.
**Verdict:** **selected** with a key adaptation: the verdict is presented to the human only when at least one of the must-meet checks fails OR the iteration count is > 1. Single-pass slugs that pass all must-meets bypass the verdict prompt automatically (Pattern A from prior chat).

### Option: `--quick` variant of `cortex-experiment open` for micro-experiments
**Pros:** Sub-hour appetite testing; minimal ceremony; composes with existing learning-contract format; gives the user a real "little tests" primitive matching the brief's wording.
**Cons:** Two interaction modes for one skill; risks confusion about when to use which.
**Verdict:** **selected.** Single flag, defaults the optional fields, requires only `hypothesis` and `appetite`.

### Option: Concrete reframe-triggers declared in the clarify brief itself
**Pros:** Solves the dormant-trigger problem mechanically — the brief declares "if research finds X, reframe me" in advance; the new dossier mechanically checks against the list; no soft-prose LLM judgment needed; matches ADR's "changes that should trigger reevaluation" field.
**Cons:** Adds a section to the brief template; requires foresight at brief-writing time.
**Verdict:** **selected.** New section in clarify brief: `## Reframe Triggers` — list of concrete, observable signals that should trigger reclarify. Research mechanically checks them at Phase 4 instead of asking the LLM to judge.

## Recommendations

**Scope this slug to three gap closures + two convention adoptions. Defer the remaining six gaps to follow-up slugs.**

### Primary recommendations (in priority order)

1. **Add `## Reframe Triggers` section to the clarify brief template.** Each trigger is a concrete observable signal: "If research finds that assumption A is contradicted," "If research surfaces a new hard constraint not in Constraints section," "If the goal sentence is not coherent with the dossier findings." `/cortex-research` Phase 4 mechanically checks the dossier against this list and writes `reclarify_required: true` if any trigger fires. **This is the single highest-leverage change** because it operationalizes the dormant gate without adding new infrastructure — it shifts the trigger from soft LLM judgment ("does this feel like a reframe?") to mechanical check ("did finding F1 contradict assumption A1?").

2. **Add `current-understanding.md` artifact per slug.** Lives at `docs/cortex/research/{slug}/current-understanding.md`. Single living document, written by `/cortex-research` at the end of each pass and updated (not overwritten) on subsequent passes. Sections: (a) what we currently know (durable findings with source links), (b) what we currently believe but haven't verified (provisional, marked as such), (c) what we still don't know (open questions with type/severity/resolution_path from the structured register), (d) iteration history table (one row per dossier produced for this slug, with reframe trigger if any). The human queries this doc when they want "what do I currently understand?" — they do not read every dossier separately.

3. **Add `--quick` flag to `/cortex-experiment open`** for micro-experiments. Defaults: `appetite: 30m`, status `open`, no Risks/Dependencies/Expected-Learning sections required, only `hypothesis` and `learning_threshold` mandatory. Closes with a simple `outcome: viable | not-viable | inconclusive` instead of the full 4-decision matrix. Designed for sub-hour tests inside a research pass. The full `cortex-experiment open` remains for heavier hypothesis testing.

### Convention adoptions (no new code, just doc + template changes)

4. **Adopt ADR-style status field on all clarify briefs and dossiers.** Add `status:` to YAML frontmatter with values `proposed | accepted | superseded`. When a new iteration is written, the prior file's status flips to `superseded` with a `superseded_by:` link. This gives readers an unambiguous signal of which artifact is current.

5. **Apply the iteration-2-of-this-slug pattern as the standard for reframings.** The frontmatter fields `iteration:`, `supersedes:`, `informed_by:`, `reframe_reason:` become required in the brief template when iteration > 1. They are optional/absent for iteration 1.

### Explicit deferrals (do NOT do in this slug)

- **Fact extraction pipeline (dossier → facts.jsonl).** Useful but can be added later as a separate slug. The current-understanding.md doc already gives the human a queryable surface.
- **Iteration history log per slug as a separate artifact.** The iteration history table in current-understanding.md is sufficient for now; a separate `iteration-log.md` is duplicative.
- **Uncertainty resolution tracker (writing back to open-questions.md).** Activate the structured register passively first via the current-understanding.md "open questions" section; auto-resolution writeback is a follow-up.
- **Dossier consolidation skill.** Current-understanding.md *is* the consolidation; a separate consolidation skill is duplicative.
- **Finding versioning within a single dossier.** ADR-style supersession at the file level (recommendation 4) is enough; intra-file versioning is overkill.
- **Stage-gate Go/Kill/Hold/Recycle verdict UI.** Worth doing eventually but not in this slug — the recommendations above already make the gate visible without requiring an interactive verdict for every slug.

### How to avoid the dormant-loop failure mode

- **Pilot on one slug first (this one).** The clarify-research-loop slug is itself the working example — its iteration-2 brief uses the new frontmatter convention; its concept dossier (this one) uses the new dossier conventions. The spec for this slug must produce a current-understanding.md *for itself* as part of the deliverable.
- **The new flow must be the default, not an opt-in flag.** The dormant gate failed partly because it required someone to remember to invoke it. `/cortex-research` Phase 4 reframe-trigger check should run unconditionally; current-understanding.md should be written unconditionally.
- **The trigger must be mechanical, not LLM-judged.** This is the lesson from the dormant gate. Concrete checks against declared triggers cannot be skipped by inattention.
- **Require the spec for this slug to migrate the iteration-2 frontmatter convention into `templates/cortex/clarify-brief.md`.** Without template-level adoption, no other slug will use the convention and we recreate the "one slug uses it" problem.

## Adjacent Findings

- **Architecture Decision Records explicitly recommend recording "changes in product context that should trigger the team to reevaluate the decision."** This is Martin Fowler's published guidance — a near-exact match for the user's "reframe triggers" need. Cortex's reframe triggers section in the clarify brief can be lifted directly from this pattern. Why this matters: this is not a Cortex novelty; it's a 20-year-old, widely-adopted convention with proven adoption in industry. Naming the new section after the established ADR pattern reduces design-novelty risk and makes adoption easier (anyone who has written an ADR already understands it). Source: https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ArchitectureDecisionRecord.html

- **Russ Poldrack's "Better Code, Better Science" Jupyter best practices: shorter notebooks beat monolithic ones, and the "restart-and-run-all" discipline is the only way to confirm reproducibility.** Translated to Cortex: many small dossiers (one per research pass, one per micro-experiment) feeding a synthesized current-understanding view is structurally sounder than one giant aggregated dossier. Why this matters: this validates the per-phase dossier model Cortex already has and argues against adding a "consolidate everything into one giant dossier" skill. The gap is in synthesis (current-understanding.md), not in the dossier granularity. Source: https://russpoldrack.substack.com/p/best-practices-for-using-jupyter

## Open Questions

- Should `current-understanding.md` be written as a literal single doc per slug, or as a templated section that any skill can render on demand from the artifact set? (Recommendation: literal doc, written by `/cortex-research`, because of the disk-is-truth constraint and audit trail value.)
- How many reframe triggers should a typical clarify brief declare? Too few and the mechanical check is weak; too many and the brief becomes ceremonial. Working hypothesis: 3-7, one per top-level assumption, one per stated non-goal.
- Does the `--quick` micro-experiment variant need its own template file, or can it reuse `learning-contract.md` with empty fields?
- When iteration 2 of a brief is written, should the iteration 1 brief's status auto-flip to `superseded`, or stay `accepted` with both marked current? (ADR practice: prior is superseded; only one is current at any time.)
- For the current-understanding.md "provisional vs durable" distinction: how does the writer know which is which? Is it self-declared in the dossier ("this finding is provisional pending experiment"), or computed from confidence scores, or determined by source type?
- The decision-gate stopping criterion combines must-meets + iteration cap + delta threshold. Where exactly is the cap enforced — in `/cortex-research` (refusing to write a 4th dossier) or in `/cortex-spec` (refusing to advance until the human makes a Go/Kill/Hold/Recycle decision)?
- Should this slug touch the autonomy preset system to add a new gate (e.g., `epistemic_loop_active: true|false`) or is it preset-agnostic?
- Pilot question: after this slug ships, what is the metric for "the new loop is working"? Number of reframings actually performed? Survey question to the user? Audit of the next 5 slugs to see if reframe triggers fire?
- How does the new pattern interact with `/cortex-drive` autonomous mode? Drive currently auto-advances through phases; the new mechanical reframe-check should integrate with that, but the interaction needs design.
- The codebase audit identified 9 gaps total. This dossier defers 6. The deferred gaps are listed but not prioritized for follow-up — should the spec for this slug also produce a forward-looking roadmap document for the deferred work?
