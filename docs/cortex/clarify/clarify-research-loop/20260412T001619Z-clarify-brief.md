# Clarify Brief: clarify-research-loop (iteration 2)

**Slug:** clarify-research-loop
**Timestamp:** 20260412T001619Z
**Status:** draft
**Complexity:** complex

> **Iteration 2 of N** — supersedes `20260411T234737Z-clarify-brief.md`. The original frame ("build a convergence gate") was invalidated by concept research showing the gate already exists but is dormant, AND by a subsequent owner reframing toward an epistemic-loop philosophy. Iteration 1 brief and the concept dossier are preserved on disk; this iteration cites them as informing context, not replacing them.

---

## Idea

> Verbatim from owner, 2026-04-12:

I'm looking to refactor this process a bit to be a bit more epistemologically interesting as a human-in-the-loop observer. I want to use research to learn things — and then maybe understand my original understanding was misguided and so I reclarify — but not lose the research. And also during research we are surfacing new things we didn't think about, so we could be blending things. What's the optimal way of doing this to stay consistent? So we have little loops, and we run little tests and stuff and build things to decision gate. It's a bit of a shift of viewpoint.

---

## Goal

Cortex's pre-spec phase becomes an **epistemic learning loop**: a workflow where research is durable knowledge that accumulates across iterations, reframings are first-class events that preserve (not erase) prior learning, newly-surfaced discoveries blend into a coherent and queryable "current understanding" of the slug, small bounded tests close uncertainty cheaply, and an explicit decision gate marks the moment commitment to a spec is justified — with the human-as-observer always able to ask "what do I currently know about this slug?" and get a single coherent answer.

---

## Non-Goals

- Not a project management tool or Gantt-chart system
- Not replacing `/cortex-spec` — spec still exists; it just gets invoked later, with more learning behind it, and is often smaller as a result
- Not enforcing a fixed loop structure — the loop should be sized to the question (some slugs need 1 pass, some need 5; the system should not impose either)
- Not building a knowledge graph or semantic memory layer (the existing facts.jsonl + simple Markdown is enough)
- Not eliminating commitment — there is still a moment of "we have learned enough; commit to spec or kill the slug." Indecision is itself a failure mode this work must prevent
- Not retrofitting all closed slugs — this is a forward-looking change, applies to slugs started after the new mechanism ships
- Not removing or replacing the existing dormant `reclarify_required` / `cortex-experiment` machinery — it should be **subsumed** into the new model, not deleted
- Not solving the multi-slug case — this work is about coherent learning *within one slug*, not across slugs (cross-slug learning is a separate concern)

---

## Constraints

- [owner-intent] No code without contract
- [owner-intent] Validators must run
- [owner-intent] Disk is truth — every preserved learning must be a repo artifact
- [owner-intent] GSD owns execution
- [owner-intent] No sycophantic evals
- **Research findings must accumulate, not be overwritten** when a slug reframes. A reframe is a delta event, not a reset event
- **Reframings must be explicit and traceable** — every iteration cites the specific findings and reasoning that motivated it (the iteration-2 frontmatter of *this* brief is the working example)
- **Little tests must be cheap and bounded** — appetite measured in minutes-to-hours, not days. They must compose with the existing `cortex-experiment` learning-contract pattern, not duplicate it
- **The system must distinguish provisional knowledge from durable learning** — "I think Y" and "I learned X (evidence: Z)" cannot be conflated in the artifact schema, or the human loses the ability to audit their own beliefs
- **Coherence is a queryable property** — the human can ask "what do I currently understand about this slug?" and get one coherent answer, not a stack of conflicting dossiers
- **The decision gate must be deterministic and auditable** — "I think we're ready" is not a passing criterion; concrete resolved-uncertainty signals are
- **Iteration must be bounded** — quality-based stopping is unstable per Shape Up's empirical observation; combine with structural caps (e.g., max iterations + appetite)

---

## Assumptions

- The owner values epistemic honesty over delivery speed — being wrong and learning is preferable to shipping fast on a bad frame
- Most insights worth preserving come from research surprises (things not in the original brief), not from confirming the brief
- The current artifact granularity (one brief per iteration, one dossier per research pass) is roughly right — the gap is in *blending* and *querying* across them, not in granularity
- The existing `cortex-experiment` skill is the right vehicle for "little tests" — it just needs micro-sizing (smaller appetite, lighter contract) and an explicit invocation pattern from inside the loop
- A "current understanding" surface can be a single living Markdown doc per slug, synthesized incrementally — not a graph database or vector index
- The human-on-the-loop has limited attention and will not read N dossiers; the surface they actually consult is the "current understanding" doc plus the latest reframe-check verdict
- This work changes the philosophy of the pre-spec phase but does not require touching post-spec phases (execute/validate/repair/assure are downstream and unaffected)
- The 5-type question taxonomy from iteration 1 still applies and is a good substrate for tracking what's been answered vs what's still open
- The owner expects the system to **push back** when they're being epistemically lazy (e.g., wanting to spec without resolving an obvious frame question) — politeness is a failure mode

## Open Questions

- What's the minimal artifact set to support "preserved + blendable" research — single growing dossier per slug, append-only findings log, or a living `current-understanding.md` synthesized from the artifact set?
- How does a new clarify iteration cite and inherit findings from prior research without losing the audit trail or creating duplicate sources of truth?
- What does a "little test" look like concretely in this loop — minimal experiment, smoke check, single API ping, single LLM prompt, single paper read? How does it differ from the existing `cortex-experiment` learning-contract (which is heavier)?
- How do scientific notebooks (Jupyter, lab notebooks), research journals, and OODA-loop practitioners structure cumulative learning across reframings — and what makes them work or fail in practice?
- Compare three blending strategies for new findings: (a) overwriting brief with new findings, (b) append-only findings log per slug, (c) versioned brief iterations with explicit cross-references. Which preserves epistemic honesty best?
- What constitutes a "decision gate" in this model, and what is the minimum set of resolved uncertainties that justifies passing one to `/cortex-spec`?
- How do we keep the human-as-observer from drowning in artifact churn while still preserving everything? What's the right surface for "show me what I currently understand about this slug?"
- Does the system need an explicit distinction between provisional knowledge ("I think Y") and durable learning ("I learned X, here's the evidence") — and if so, where does it live in the artifact schema?

## Next Research Steps

1. **[codebase]** Audit how `cortex-experiment`, `cortex-stash`, the dormant `reclarify_required` machinery, and the structured uncertainty register could compose into the "little loop" primitive — the goal is to subsume, not duplicate
2. **[landscape]** Survey scientific notebook patterns (Jupyter as a notebook genre, not just a tool), wet-lab notebook practices, the OODA loop literature, and structured research-journal templates. What makes cumulative learning across reframings actually work in human practice?
3. **[mechanism]** Investigate three "current understanding" representations and pick one: (a) a single living `current-understanding.md` per slug, (b) computed-on-demand from dossier set, (c) append-only findings log with view layer
4. **[factual]** Define concrete examples of "little test" for non-code questions: reading 1 paper, calling 1 API, prompting 1 LLM, hand-classifying 5 examples, writing 10 lines of throwaway code. The threshold is appetite < 1 hour and produces an artifact
5. **[comparison]** Compare three blending strategies (overwrite / append / versioned-with-refs) on the criteria: epistemic honesty, audit trail, cognitive load on human, implementation cost
6. **[factual]** Define decision-gate criteria: the minimum set of resolved uncertainties (mapped to the 5-type taxonomy) that justifies passing to `/cortex-spec`. Should this be per-slug-configurable or universal?
7. **[mechanism]** Design the "show me what I currently understand" surface — likely a single command (`/cortex-current` or `/cortex-state --slug`) that produces a one-page synthesis. What does that page contain?
8. **[factual]** Specify how provisional vs durable knowledge gets marked in artifacts — frontmatter field on findings? Separate sections? Naming convention?
9. **[mechanism]** How does the loop handle a finding that contradicts a prior dossier's finding? Both can be on disk; the "current understanding" must reconcile them. What's the reconciliation rule?
