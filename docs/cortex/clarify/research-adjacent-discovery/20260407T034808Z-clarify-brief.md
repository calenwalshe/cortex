## Idea

The Cortex research phase currently answers the questions it's asked — it follows the clarify brief's open questions and next research steps. But it doesn't proactively surface adjacent topics, concepts, risks, or opportunities that the user should be aware of but hasn't thought to ask about. The research phase should educate, not just inform. It should say "you asked about X, and here's your answer, but you should also know about Y and Z because they'll affect your decisions."

---

## Goal

Extend the Cortex research phase to proactively identify and surface adjacent knowledge — concepts, risks, opportunities, prior art, and domain context that the user didn't ask about but should be aware of to make better decisions.

---

## Non-Goals

- Not replacing the directed research output — adjacency discovery is additive, not a substitute
- Not building a general-purpose learning/tutoring system
- Not doing speculative "here's everything tangentially related" brain dumps — findings must be decision-relevant
- Not requiring the user to read adjacency findings before proceeding (advisory, not blocking)

---

## Assumptions

- Users don't know what they don't know — the highest-value research findings are often things the user didn't think to ask
- The clarify brief's open questions and constraints provide enough context to infer what adjacent topics are relevant
- LLMs are good at identifying "adjacent possible" knowledge given a well-framed problem
- A small number of high-quality adjacent findings (3-5) is more valuable than an exhaustive list

---

## Open Questions

- What's the right heuristic for deciding something is "adjacent and relevant" vs. "tangential noise"?
- Should adjacent findings be a section in the existing research dossier, or a separate companion artifact?
- How do we evaluate quality of adjacency recommendations — what does a good one look like vs. a bad one?
- Should the system explain *why* each adjacent finding matters (relevance justification) or just surface it?
- Can we use the clarify brief's non-goals and constraints as a filter — if it's already excluded, don't surface it?
- How do existing research tools (Perplexity, Tavily, Gemini) handle "related topics" and can we leverage that?
