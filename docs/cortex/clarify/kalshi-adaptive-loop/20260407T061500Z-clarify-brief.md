## Idea

Increase the reps of the kalshi prediction system so it self-evolves to become more adapted at forecasting. $10/day in bets. The structure of cortex itself adjusts bit by bit based on what the failure analysis reveals — prompts, research strategies, market selection heuristics all adapt. Also expand to betting on weirder and weirder surfaces beyond weather — broader forecasting domains.

---

## Goal

Build a closed-loop adaptive system where kalshi-calibration-harness failure analysis (F1-F10 taxonomy, Brier scores, calibration curves) automatically drives modifications to the cortex pipeline — research prompts, calibration heuristics, market selection — producing measurable improvement in forecasting accuracy over successive 50-prediction batches, while progressively expanding to harder prediction surfaces.

---

## Non-Goals

- Not building a profit-maximizing trading system — P&L remains a signal, not the objective
- Not fine-tuning or training LLMs — adaptations happen at the pipeline/prompt level, not model weights
- Not replacing human judgment on what surfaces to expand into — human picks the next category, system optimizes within it
- Not building a general-purpose AutoML/hyperparameter-tuning framework — adaptations are specific to the forecasting pipeline
- Not automating position sizing (Kelly criterion) until calibration baseline is established (500+ predictions)

## Assumptions

- kalshi-calibration-harness contracts 002-004 will be completed, providing the pipeline (research, compare, post-mortem, scoring) this slug builds on
- The F1-F10 failure taxonomy produces actionable signal — failure codes cluster in ways that point to specific pipeline improvements
- Prompt/strategy-level adaptations can meaningfully improve calibration without model fine-tuning
- $10/day provides enough volume for statistically detectable improvement (~2-3 bets/day at $3-5 each)
- Kalshi has sufficient market diversity across categories to support surface expansion (weather -> politics -> economics -> entertainment)
- 50-prediction batches are large enough to detect calibration changes above noise

## Open Questions

- What is the adaptation mechanism — does the system propose pipeline changes for human approval, or does it auto-apply changes below a risk threshold?
- How do we measure whether an adaptation helped — A/B testing (run old and new pipeline on same markets), or sequential comparison (before/after on different markets)?
- What is the surface expansion ladder — which categories after weather, and what criteria determine readiness to expand?
- How do we prevent overfitting adaptations to a specific surface (e.g., weather-specific heuristics that hurt on politics)?
- What is the minimum detectable effect size — how many predictions do we need to confidently say a pipeline change improved calibration?
- Should adaptations be per-surface (weather pipeline vs politics pipeline) or universal (one pipeline that handles all surfaces)?
- How do we handle the cold-start problem on a new surface — no failure data exists yet to guide adaptations?
